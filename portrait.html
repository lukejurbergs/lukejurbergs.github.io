The Work

This was the first time I used colored pencils beyond filling in shapes and instead tried to accurately depict something real. Skin tone in colored pencil doesn't start with peach. It starts with dark purple, then cool red, then peach on top, then light white blended over that. You build the color up layer by layer, line by line, until the layers stop looking like layers and start looking like skin.

The most important part of the project was defying my teacher to create my vision. My teacher told me to draw my eyes simply, a solid color with a few highlights and shadows. Instead I went home, spent multiple nights watching YouTube tutorials, filling sketchbooks with iris studies, and working out how to make a detailed, realistic iris in colored pencil. I came back to class and started drawing it the way I'd figured out.

It started looking like a flower, abstract, nothing like a real eye. My teacher stopped me and said it didn't look realistic. She said it looked inaccurate. But I kept going anyway, because I could see where it was headed even if she couldn't yet. By the end, the iris had real structure, layers of color, and tiny visible patterns in the pigment. It looked like an eye. My teacher loved it and even ended up giving me extra credit.

What I Learned

01
Trusting your vision when no one else can see it yet. The iris looked wrong for a long time before it looked right. That's always how it works with things built in layers. If you stop when it looks wrong, you never get to when it looks right. This same message is something I've carried throughout my engineering experience
02
Color is built, not applied. This was my first time using colored pencils in this way, and it taught me a lot about how to create the best art. You don't just use one color and then shade with black and white on top. Instead, you build colors layer by layer to get the perfect coloring. Learning this would be very important for my future success in painting and drawing.
