The Work

This was the first time I used colored pencils to try to accurately depict something real and make it look like a person. Skin tone in colored pencil doesn't start with peach. It starts with dark purple, then cool red, then peach on top, then light white blended over that. You build the color up layer by layer, line by line, until the layers stop looking like layers and start looking like skin.

The part I spent the most time on was the eye. My teacher told me to draw it simply, a solid color with a few highlights and shadows. Instead I went home, spent multiple nights watching YouTube tutorials, filling sketchbooks with iris studies, and working out how to make a detailed, realistic iris in colored pencil. I came back to class and started drawing it the way I'd figured out.

It started looking like a flower, abstract, nothing like a real eye. My teacher stopped me and said it didn't look realistic. I kept going anyway, because I could see where it was headed even if she couldn't yet. By the end the iris had real structure, layers of color, tiny visible patterns in the pigment. It looked like an eye. My teacher loved it and gave me extra credit.

What I Learned

01
Trusting your vision when no one else can see it yet. The iris looked wrong for a long time before it looked right. That's always how it works with things built in layers. If you stop when it looks wrong, you never get to when it looks right.
02
Color is built, not applied. Skin in colored pencil is four colors minimum, none of them peach first. The purple underneath is what makes the warm tones above it look warm. You can't shortcut the layers.
