The Magazine

The Modern Palette was an art magazine I made in LASA's Electronic Magazine class. As a freshman, I created a four-page feature article on architecture as art, and a two-page infographic on European architectural styles, as well as additional pieces like the cover, table of contents, a game page, etc.

The Covers

Front and back covers of the Spring 2024 issue, being the cover designer for a magazine that encompassed so many different art forms from music to film to architecture, it was tricky. I landed on a very abstract, colorful design.

Artistry In Architecture — Feature Article

My article covers three Austin architects with completely different views on whether architecture is art. Maanasa Nathan from Furman + Keil Architects argues architecture is art, Katrina Winsko from Andersson Wise Architects talks about balancing emotion with function, and Chris Daemmrich, architect and social justice advocate, disagrees with both, arguing architecture isn't purely about art but also utility.

Getting the interviews meant reaching out to the firms myself, arranging the meetings, going to their offices, asking the questions, taking photos of their workspaces, and collecting press packages. The writing, layout, and photo editing were all done independently, without adult guidance.

When writing the magazine, I carefully tried not to give a single conclusion but let all three opinions exist in a discussion. Three smart people who do this work every day see it completely differently, and collapsing them into a conclusion would have been dishonest.

Diverse Designs — Architecture in Europe Infographic

A two-page infographic I created mapping eight architectural styles across Europe, including Gothic, Modern, Post-Modern, Victorian, Tudor, Colonial, Contemporary, and Bauhaus. Each style gets a timeline, key characteristics, and an illustrated building. A color-coded map shows which countries favor which style, based on a survey of citizens across the continent by a researcher.

The Sketch

Hand-drawn planning sketch for the infographic. I made the full layout in pencil and colored pencil before going to digital. One aspect I didn't keep in mind during my initial sketch was margins (for printing) and focus (I emphasized the style's number rather than the style's title). Buildings, map, numbered styles, sources, and editorial framing all worked out on paper first.

What I Learned

01
Getting access is a skill. I sent cold emails to architecture firms. Getting them to say yes, showing up, conducting the interview, taking photos, and leaving with usable material was tricky and took a lot of confidence and time. It was especially difficult as a freshman, but I managed and was successful.
02
The best stories don't have one answer. Nathan says architecture is art. Daemmrich says it isn't. Winsko is somewhere in the middle. Letting those three coexist in one article without collapsing them into a conclusion made it more honest and more interesting to read, and I use the same techniques of letting uncertainty linger in so much of my art, from Paranoid to written stories.
03
Design and writing are both about space and focus. Laying out a magazine spread means deciding what goes where, what size, and how to get the reader's eye to jump to the right places. Same decisions as filmmaking, just at a smaller scale.
04
Sketch first. The infographic only worked because I'd already solved the layout problems on paper, like where to place the bibliography. That meant the digital version was massively easier, even when facing unexpected issues like printing margins.
