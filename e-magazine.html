The Article

The article covers the question of whether architecture is art, using three architects in Austin with completely different answers. Maanasa Nathan from Furman and Keil Architects argues yes, drawing parallels between architectural movements and art history. Katrina Winsko from Andersson Wise Architects talks about the balance between evoking emotion and building for function. Chris Daemmrich, an architect and educator, disagrees with both and says architecture lives outside art because art doesn't need to be useful.

The entire project was done without adult guidance. Getting the interviews meant reaching out to the firms myself, arranging the meetings, going to their offices, asking the questions, taking photos of their workspaces, and collecting press packages. The writing, layout, and photo editing were all done independently.

The article ended up being two full pages of the magazine, including photos I took at the firms. The story doesn't land on a single answer to whether architecture is art, which was intentional. Three smart people who do this work every day see it completely differently.

What I Learned

01
Getting access is a skill. Sending cold emails to architecture firms, getting them to say yes, showing up, conducting the interview, taking photos, and leaving with usable material. None of that is automatic. All of it is learnable.
02
The best stories don't have one answer. Nathan says architecture is art. Daemmrich says it isn't. Winsko is somewhere in the middle. Letting those three coexist in one article, without collapsing them into a conclusion, made it more interesting to read.
03
Design and writing are both about space. Laying out a magazine spread means deciding what goes where, what size, what breathes and what doesn't. Same decisions as a building, just at a smaller scale.
