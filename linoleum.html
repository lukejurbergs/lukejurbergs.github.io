The Work

The design is a tortoise, and instead of a normal shell, the shell is a small ecosystem of mushrooms and ferns growing across its back. The idea was that the tortoise carries its whole world with it. Slow, ancient, and its home is also its garden.

The project was nearly a disaster. When the deadline arrived I had barely touched the linoleum. To finish it I gave up my lunch period for 10 days straight and carved during the break, every day, until it was done. Not ideal, but it worked, and it taught me what I'm capable of when the deadline is actually real.

Art 1 grading for this project worked through submission. First attempt: 60. They graded what you had done, and you kept working. Second attempt: 75. Third: 90. Fourth: 95. That structure forced everyone to keep improving rather than settling. I kept submitting until I hit 95.

What I Learned

01
Iteration over a grade structure that forces it is genuinely useful. Submitting at 60 and having to keep working sounds brutal. It's better than submitting once and being done. By the fourth pass the carving was actually good.
02
Every carved line is permanent. Unlike drawing where you can erase, linoleum is additive subtraction. You remove material and it stays removed. Every cut has to be intentional because you can't undo it, which makes you slow down and think before you cut.
