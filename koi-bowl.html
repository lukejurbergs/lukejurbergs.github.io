The Work

Made as part of the Austin Empty Bowl Project, which connects art students with hunger relief. The bowls are sold, and the funds go to people who actually need food. The idea is that when you eat from the bowl, you're meant to think about those who don't have one. It's a good prompt to make something that earns that weight.

The bowl is hand-sculpted red clay with a footed base. I carved koi fish and flowers directly into the exterior walls, then painted glaze — blue over the whole body, with white and additional colors on the flowers and koi detail. A yin-yang sits on the exterior base, mirrored by one carved into the interior floor. The koi circle in one continuous loop around the outside, which mirrors the yin-yang at the bottom. Balance, motion, things looping back into themselves.

The glazes overlapped in places during firing and blurred the outlines of some of the koi. It made it look messier than I'd planned. But looking at it now, the imperfection feels right for an object designed around the idea that you keep seeking peace even when things are messy. A bowl made for people going through difficult things probably shouldn't look perfect.

Before the Glaze

The raw clay version — before any blue glaze was applied. The carved flowers and koi are clear here, and the koi interior outlines on the base floor are much more legible. The glaze unified and enriched the surface but also bled into some of the fine lines during firing.

The Other Clay Project — Relaxalotl

Made around the same time, the Relaxalotl is the contrast piece. Where the bowl is about stillness, weight, and function, the Relaxalotl is pure invention. It's a fantasy creature — a long snaking axolotl with a mermaid-style tail, fins running down its body, and the signature axolotl head tentacles. Bright pink clay body, deep red fins done in needle felting, pink felted tentacles, large black eyes. Everything about it is made up and joyful.

The bowl used glaze. The Relaxalotl used paint and incorporated needle felting for the fins, gills, and tentacles — a totally different set of materials and a totally different emotional register. Both were made in the same class, same semester. One went to a family who needed a bowl. The other came with a branded box and care instructions.

What I Learned

01
Ceramics does not forgive the same way drawing does. Once the glaze fires it's permanent. The overlapping happened during the kiln and there was nothing to do about it. That's a different kind of acceptance than dealing with a mistake you can fix.
02
A project with a purpose outside of itself is easier to care about. Making something for the Austin Empty Bowl Project felt different from making something for a grade. That difference shows up in the work.
03
Mixed media opens things up. The Relaxalotl worked because needle felting gave the fins a texture that clay alone couldn't. Paint gave different control than glaze. Using the right material for each part of a sculpture — rather than forcing one medium to do everything — is its own skill.
