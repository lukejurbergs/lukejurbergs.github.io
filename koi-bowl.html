The Work

Made as part of the Austin Empty Bowl Project, which connects art students with hunger relief. The bowls are sold, and the funds go to people who actually need food. The idea is that when you eat from the bowl, you're meant to think about those who don't have one. It's a good prompt to make something that earns that weight.

The bowl is hand-sculpted red clay. After firing, I painted it blue and used glaze to add koi fish circling the outside rim, flowers along the walls, and yin-yang symbols on both the interior and exterior base. The koi circle in one continuous loop, which mirrors the yin-yang at the bottom. Balance, motion, things looping back into themselves.

The glazes overlapped in places during firing and blurred the outlines of some of the koi. It made it look messier than I'd planned. But looking at it now, the imperfection feels right for an object designed around the idea that you keep seeking peace even when things are messy. A bowl made for people going through difficult things probably shouldn't look perfect.

What I Learned

01
Ceramics does not forgive the same way drawing does. Once the glaze fires it's permanent. The overlapping happened during the kiln and there was nothing to do about it. That's a different kind of acceptance than dealing with a mistake you can fix.
02
A project with a purpose outside of itself is easier to care about. Making something for the Austin Empty Bowl Project felt different from making something for a grade. That difference shows up in the work.
