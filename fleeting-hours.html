The Film

Fleeting Hours is a single-take day-in-the-life micro film, built around one piece of music. The film opens with me playing Bach's Prelude and Fugue No.2 from the Well Tempered Clavier on piano. The music keeps playing as the film cuts through the rest of the day: pickleball outside, work on the computer, math on the laptop. It ends back at the piano, the song winding down, and then sleep.

I was learning that Bach piece with my piano teacher Angelo Lembesis at the time, which is part of why it felt right to use. The music doesn't stop between scenes. It becomes the thread connecting all these different parts of a day that don't obviously belong together. Piano, sport, school, math, back to piano. That's the point.

This was one of my first films, made individually for AVP class. No crew, no director's notes, just me and a camera and a piece of music I was in the middle of learning. It's a simple film and intentionally so. The restraint was the assignment, and it turned out to be good training.

What I Learned

01
Restraint is its own technique. No plot, no dialogue, no characters other than me. The constraint of a micro film forced every shot to do more work. Picking the right angle for the pickleball scene mattered because there was no story to fall back on.
02
Music does the editing for you if you let it. Cutting to the beat of Bach across wildly different scenes and having it feel like one piece taught me something about how rhythm unifies footage that has nothing else in common.
03
Starting simple is how you learn to want more. Fleeting Hours is a day in my life. Perfectly Alone, made later, is a story about that life. Paranoid is fiction about the world. The progression happened because this one worked.
