VISUAL
WORK

Engineering teaches you how things work. Art forces you to think about why they matter. These projects live in both of those worlds at once. Some are films, some are sculptures, some are journalism, and one is just a bedroom door. They're all the same impulse: make something that didn't exist before, and care enough about it to get it right.

Paranoid won an award and taught me what leadership actually is. The Berlin Wall diorama won 1st place at a regional competition and 3rd at state. The rest taught me things that don't fit into award categories.

↗ Short Film  ·  Director, Writer, Actor
PARANOID

A woman living alone starts noticing objects moving, things disappearing. She tells her landlord's son, who calls her dramatic. She tells her friend, who blames stress. She installs security cameras. The attic camera goes offline. She goes up herself. The film is about what happens when no one believes you, and then it's too late for it to matter.

↗ Historical Diorama  ·  1st Place Sprachfest  ·  3rd Place Texas State
BERLIN WALL DIORAMA

About the Bethke brothers' 1989 escape from East Germany by ultralight aircraft. The west side is bright with painted murals including the brotherly kiss. The east side is gray, littered, windows bricked up near the wall. The death strip between them has a watchtower, anti-vehicle trenches with real soil, barbed wire from floral wire, a raked sand strip. Above everything, a model of the Microlight Fox II is suspended on a dowel, camouflage painted with red Soviet stars. Built from scrap acoustic foam jigsawed into shape, cold brew can for the nose cone, LEGO motorcycle wheels combined with 3D printed parts.

↗ Colored Pencil  ·  Art 1
SELF
PORTRAIT

Skin tone in colored pencil starts with dark purple, not peach. Then cool red, then peach, then white blended on top. The iris took multiple nights of YouTube tutorials and practice sketches. My teacher told me it didn't look realistic. I kept going. She gave me extra credit when it was finished.
Best print: high contrast ink, tortoise with mushroom shell
↗ Printmaking  ·  Art 1
LINOLEUM
CARVING

A tortoise whose shell is a garden of mushrooms and ferns. Carved during 10 consecutive lunch periods when the deadline hit. Submitted four times with grades of 60, 75, 90, then 95. Each submission required more work. The structure forced better results than any single-attempt grading would have.
Top-down shot of bowl interior, koi circling, yin-yang base
↗ Ceramics  ·  Austin Empty Bowl Project
KOI FISH
BOWL

Hand-sculpted red clay bowl for the Austin Empty Bowl Project. Koi fish loop around the exterior in glaze. Yin-yangs painted on the interior and exterior base. The glazes overlapped during firing and blurred some of the outlines. It's a better metaphor for an object designed around seeking peace in the middle of mess.

↗ Journalism  ·  Design  ·  Spring 2024
E MAGAZINE
ARCHITECTURE

A two-page feature on architecture as art, with three architects who all answer the question differently. Cold-emailed the firms, arranged the meetings, showed up at their offices, asked the questions, took the photos, and wrote the article without adult guidance. Published Spring 2024.
Full-length door in room, showing the complete painted design
↗ Painting  ·  Room
MINECRAFT
DOOR

Bedroom door painted as a Minecraft spruce door. Done alone. During COVID, Minecraft was where engineering started: entire medieval cities, redstone bunkers with piston doors, airlocks, automated lighting. The door is the first thing seen coming home. It's a reminder of where the building impulse started and where it went.
