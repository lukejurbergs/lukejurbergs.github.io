The Mission

Build a device that starts in Box 1, travels to Box 2, picks up a donut, travels to Box 3, and places the donut on a cone — autonomously, repeatably, and under $5 total cost with less than 3 joules of energy. No adhesives, no disposables. Presented to a board of engineers at the end of the semester.

This was my main engineering project of freshman year — 18 weeks, 130 hours of planning, CAD, fabrication, testing, and rebuilding.

How It Worked

SEQ
The Sequence

A pin releases. An arm falls. A hook grabs the donut. Counterweighted weights create imbalance that raises the arm. A cam-follower pin triggers claw release at exactly the right point over the cone. A scissorlift extends to bring the donut down onto the cone. All mechanical. All timed by geometry, not electronics.

What I Learned

01
Cost allocation. We spent too much of the $5 budget on a complex popsicle stick system and not enough on the base. Every project has budget tradeoffs — ignoring them is a design error, not a luck problem.
02
Material selection matters more than you think. Popsicle sticks introduced inconsistent friction everywhere they appeared. Precision wood cutouts from the laser cutter would have cost the same and worked 10x better. We learned this lesson late.
03
Real engineering deliverables. First time creating a proper BOM, MOM, CAD model, and Design Report. The board presentation made it real — this wasn't a classroom exercise, it was accountable engineering.
